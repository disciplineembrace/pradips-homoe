MILLEFOLIUM

Yarrow Compositae

INTRODUCTION

Another name for this plant is ‘Noseb-leed’, a
very appropriate term because of its great
tendency to produce haemorrhage, not only from
the nose, but from every orifice and of the human
system. Its medicinal proper-ties were known to
the ancients. It is mentioned in Homer’s [liad as
being utilised by Achilles to heal the wounds of
his soldiers and hence it is called Achillea
millefolium.

CLINICAL

Cough, Convulsions, Epilepsy, Haemorrhages,

Hysteria, Leucorrhoea, Vertigo.

1) Haemorrhages : from all the orifices of the body ; from wounds or of mechanical
origin ; painless haemorrhage without fever ; blood is bright red and fluid.

2) Profusely bleeding wound especially after a fall.
3) Vertigo when moving slowly, but not when taking violent exercise.

4) Cough: Causation : Suppressed menses or haemorrhoids, after a fall from a
height, after violent exertion.

Character of cough : Cough with raising of blood daily at 4 p.m. (Lyco.)
Concomitant : Cough with oppression and palpitation.

5) Menses :
Time : Early
Quantity : Profuse 4
Duration : Protracted
Character : Suppressed with colic pain in abdomen. Blood is bright red & fluid.
6) Leucorrhoea of Children from atony (Cal., Caulo.)

7) Ailments from : Falls (from height), Overexertion. Lifting. Suppressed lochia.
Suppressed menses. Suppressed haemorrhoids etc.

In haemorrhage, Millefolium canbe.compared with Erige-ron, Ipceac, Nitric
Acid, Phosphorus & Trillium.
