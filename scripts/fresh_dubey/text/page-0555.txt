SANGUINARIA CANADENSIS

Blood Root G. BUTE Papaveraceae

-

INTRODUCTION

Blood root is used to be one of the oldest
domestic remedies. In winter season, farmers
wives would not forget to keep always blood root
in their house. In the cold winter days when the
coryza comes on, with a cold in the head, chest,
throat, then they get hold of the blood root and
make a cup of tea with it which was a routine
drug to combat all complaints from cold, Even in
this crude form it is used to break up colds.

CLINICAL

Alcholism, Asthma, Breast affection,
Bronchitis, Cancer, Catarrh, Chest affection,

Cold, Dyspepsia, Headache, Influenza, Menstrual disorders, Pneumonia, Rheumatism,
Vomiting, Whooping cough, etc.

SPHERES OF ACTION

Sanguinaria acts chiefly upon the mucous membrane, especially of the respiratory
tract. It has marked vasomotor disturbances.
PATHOGENESIS

It irritates the mucous membrane and produces catarrhal inflammation. It also
causes derangement of liver and the digestive tract.
CONSTITUTION

Since it is more or less an acute drug, no importance is attached to the constitution
as such. The patient is however, CHILLY, with psora in the background, having
tubercular diathesis.

GUIDING SYMPTOMS

1. Highly chilly patient; very much susceptible to cold, even gets affected by a change
of weather.

2. It is a right sided remedy. Right sided affections of the whole body e.g., head,
chest, lungs, arm, shoulders, ete.

3. There is CIRCUMSCRIBED REDNESS OF THE CHEEKS, especially in the
afternoon and the respiratory diseases e.g., in bronchitis, pneumonia, pulmonary
tuberculosis, etc.
