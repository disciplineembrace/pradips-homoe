TEREBINTHINA

Oil of turpentine A volatile oil

INTRODUCTION

The oil of turpentine, a clear colourless liquid
is obtained by the distillation of the exudation
from certain varieties of the pine.

Terebinthina was introduced to homoeopathy
by Hartlaub. A proving of it appeared in
Hartlaub and Trinks’ Annalen. Many symptoms
have been added from poisonings and over-
dosings in old-school practice.

Spheres of action & Pathogenesis : This remedy has a selec-tive affinity for mucous
membrances of kidneys ; bladder and urethra, also respiratory and gastrointestinal tract.
It also acts on heart, blood and skin. It checks haemorrhage from the bowels, kidneys,
bladder, lungs, uterus and anus, spe-cially when the ejected blood shows signs of

degeneration.

GUIDING SYMPTOMS :

1) Tongue : Tongue symptoms of Terebinthina are very characteristics for the selection
of a remedy than any other symptoms.

(a) Tongue smooth, glossy, red, as if deprived of pa-pillae or as if glazed. The
papillae may be elevated.

(b) The coating of tongue peels off in patches leaving bright red spots, or entire
coating cleans off suddenly.

(c) Dry and red tongue with burning sensation on tip.
2) The urine has violet i.e. sweetish odour.

3) Excessive tympanitis : abdomen is extremely sensitive to touch and distended with
flatulence.

4) Haematuria : In haematuria the character of urine is cloudy, smoky, albuminous
and blood is thoroughly, mixed with the urine. There is coffee ground sediment.
Painless haematuria with profuse, dark or black urine.

5) Violent burning and drawing pains in Kidneys, bladder and urethra (Berb., Canth,
