TRILLIUM PENDULUM

Wake Robin H. MINTON Smilaceac

INTRODUCTION

Another popular name for this plant
is Beth—root, which sufficiently signifies
its reputation as a great haemostat.
Though this remedy has not been proved
thoroughly, there is hardly any doubt as
to its capacity to control haemorrhage, be
it from nose, kidney, uterus or any other
organ.

GUIDING SYMPTOMS

1) In haemorrhage from nose, lungs, kidneys, uterus or any other organ where the
character of blood is usually pro-fuse, bright red and flow may be active or passive.

2) Menses : Every two weeks, lasting a week or longer and very profuse.

3) Flooding, with fainting ; faint, dim sight, palpitation, obstruction and noises in
the ears.

4) Menorrhagia ; Flow profuse, gushing, bright red, at least movement (Sabina); from
displaced uterus; at the climacteric; every two weeks ; dark clotted (Thlas. Ust).

5) Incipient phthisis with bloody sputa ; in advanced stages with copious, purulent
expectoration and troublesome cough.

6) Sensation as if hips & small of back were falling to pieces ; as if sacro-illiac
synchondroses were falling apart, wants to be bound tightly ; as if bone of pelvis

were broken vith haemorrhage.

RELATIONS :
Complementary—to Cale Phos in menstrual and haemorrhagic affections.
