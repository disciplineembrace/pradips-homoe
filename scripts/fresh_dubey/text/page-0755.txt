Temperament : One’s customary form or natural disposition,

(a) Bilious t. : In which it is marked by more or less general pigmentation, high blood
presssure, slow pulse, well developed muscle, strong appetite and tenacity! of
purpose.

(b) Choleric t. : Bilious t.

(c) Nervous t. : In which the subject is mentally, and physically alert, with, rapid
pulse, excitability, but not having always fixity of purpose.

(d) Leucophlegmatic t. : In which there is pallor of skin slow and shallow respiration,
sluggish circulation and a tendency to inflammation of skin and lymphatics; same
as scrofulous diathesis.

(e) Lymphatic t. : Same as leucophlegmatic t.

(f) Sanguine t. : Which has a fresh complexion; light hair and eyes; a full pulse, good
digestion, and a quick but not lasting temper.

(g) Melancholic t : One marked by emaciation, irritability, and a pessimistic out look
on the world.
