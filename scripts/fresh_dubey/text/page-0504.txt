Wild lemon

INTRODUCTION

Podophyllum grows throughout the United
States in damp, sheddy places in wood. It has
leaves having 5 to 9 lobes, large white flowers
with fruits like small lemon; hence the plant is
sometimes called wild lemon. Indian tribes use
the roots to expel worms and also drop the juice
of the root into the ear to cure deafness. In
Homoeopathy it has been introduced by our
Master, with its reputation as the VEGETABLE
MERCURY.

CLINICAL

Diarrhoea, Dysentery, Dyspepsia, Female genital organs, Fever, Flatulence, Gallstone,
Headache, Jaundice, Liver affection, Tenesmus, etc.

SPHERES OF ACTION

It acts especially upon the liver and digestive tract, has particular affinity to the
mucous membrane, more especially of the duodenum, rectum and glandular structure.

It produces irritation and excessive secretion with a characteristic jelly-like mucus
from the intestine. Acting on the liver, it causes torpidity of liver jaundice, portal
engorgement with tendency to haemorrhoids and fulness of the superficial veins.

CONSTITUTION
Physical make-up: Adapted to persons who suffer from  gastro-intestinal
derangements, especially after abuse of mercury.
Children who suffer from diarrhoea during the time of dentition in hot weather.
Temperament: Bilious temperament.
Relation with heat and cold: Affected by extremes of heat and cold.
Miasm: Psora and syphilis are in the background.
