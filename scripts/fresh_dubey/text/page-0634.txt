INTRODUCTION

Tuberculinum is prepared from the
pus of human pulmonary tubercle whereas
Bacillinum is prepared from the lung
tissue in which tubercular bacilli are
detected microscopically. CLARKE makes
no distinction between the two remedies.
He says, ONE WILL ANSWER TO THE
INDICATION OF THE OTHER ALLEN
does not apparently recognise any
difference between the two preparation in
potencies.

SPHERES OF ACTION

Tuberculinum acts on skin, lungs, gastrointestinal tract, especially, bronchi, lungs,
pleurae lymph glands, particularly those of cervical group.
PATHOGENESIS

It affects the respiratory system manifesting as bronchitis, catarrhal pneumonia,
tubercular pleurisy; the skin manifests itching eczema with scabies.

CONSTITUTION

Physical make-up: It is especailly adapted to the light complexioned, narrow-chest
subjects with blue eyes, who are tall, slim and active, but rapidly emaciate especially
children. Persons who are very precocious mentally but weak physically.

Temperament: Nervous persons with the tubercular soil.

Relation with heat and cold: Highly chilly patient takes cold easily on slight exposure
to cold air.
