MENYANTHES TRI FOLIATA

“Buckbean HAHNEYMANN Gentianaceae

INTRODUCTION

In common language it is called as ‘Buck-
bean’ or Bog—Bean ‘as it grows in boggy places.
It is a water plant and abounds in few districts,
where it is in great local repute, as a remedy for
ague. It was introduced into homoeopathy by
Hahnemann, who proved it on himself.

CLINICAL

Headache. Heart pains. Intermittent fever.
Gastric affections. Nerve—disorders.

GUIDING SYMPTOMS
1) Headache: The headache found under this remedy is very characteristic.
Location : There is pressing pain in vertex from above down wards.

Sensation : Sensation as of a heavy weight pressing upon head at every step. (Cac.,
Glon., Lach.).

Modalities : Aggravation : Ascending (Calc.)
Amelioration—During hard pressure with hands, stooping, sitting.

Concomitant: Headache is often associated with icy coldness of hands and feet (Cal.
Sep.).

2) Gastric Symptoms :
(i) Thirst: No thirst at any time,
(ii) Has desire for meat,
(iii) Ravenous hunger passing away after eating a little,
(iv) Sensation of coldness in stomach and abdomen,
(v) Distention of abdomen increased by smoking tobacco.

3) Extremities : Icey coldness of hands and feet. As soon as patient lies down, legs
jerk & twitch.

4) Anxiety ; about the heart as if some evil was impending.

5) Tension : in root of nose; in arms, hands, fingers; in skin as if of several sizes, too
small and was crowded into it by force.

6) Ailments from : Complaints from abuse of cinchona and quinine.
